### [grounded] For hourly demand with strong daily and weekly seasonality, when should I prefer a direct forecasting strategy over a recursive one?

- Skills: choosing-a-forecaster; 7.7s

You should prefer a direct forecasting strategy over a recursive one when the predictive relationship changes substantially across the forecast horizon, or when error accumulation across multi-step recursions significantly degrades accuracy at later hours. In hourly demand settings with strong daily (24-hour) and weekly (168-hour) cycles, direct models train a dedicated model for each specific lead time, preventing intermediate forecast errors from compounding.

## When to Prefer ForecasterDirect

* Error propagation is high: In a recursive setup (`ForecasterRecursive`), predictions from earlier steps become inputs for subsequent steps. Across a 24-step or 168-step horizon, small errors early on can compound and distort peak demand forecasts. `ForecasterDirect` trains separate estimators for each step, conditioning each forecast only on observed historical data.
* Predictive dynamics vary by horizon: The features driving demand 1 hour ahead (such as recent consecutive hourly lags) often differ from the features driving demand 24 or 48 hours ahead (where daily seasonal lags and calendar features dominate). A direct strategy allows each step-specific model to learn distinct weights for the features.
* The forecast horizon is fixed: If your operational setup always forecasts a specific, unchanging window (such as the next 24 hours for day-ahead scheduling), the requirement in `ForecasterDirect` to declare `steps` at initialization is not a constraint.

## Key Trade-Offs to Consider

* Computational cost: `ForecasterDirect` trains N separate estimators, where N is the number of steps in the horizon. For a 24-hour horizon it trains 24 models, and for a full weekly 168-hour horizon it trains 168 models. This increases training time and memory usage compared to the single model in `ForecasterRecursive`.
* Parallelization: Training multiple estimators can be sped up by setting the `n_jobs` argument during initialization.
* Horizon flexibility: `ForecasterRecursive` can predict any number of steps ahead at predict time with a single trained model, whereas `ForecasterDirect` is locked to the `steps` defined during initialization.
* Starting recommendation: Start with `ForecasterRecursive` as a baseline due to faster training and lower complexity. Move to `ForecasterDirect` if backtesting reveals that recursive accuracy drops off sharply as the step count increases.

* Use `skforecast.stats.calculate_lag_autocorrelation` to compute autocorrelation and partial autocorrelation (PACF) values across a range of lags.
* Focus on partial autocorrelation, which measures the direct correlation between the target and a specific past lag after removing the influence of intervening lags.
* Filter candidate lags using the asymptotic 95% white-noise threshold, calculated as `1.96 / np.sqrt(len(y_train))`. Lags with absolute partial autocorrelation exceeding this threshold serve as an informed starting set.

```python

from skforecast.stats import calculate_lag_autocorrelation

# Rank lags by absolute partial autocorrelation
lag_table = calculate_lag_autocorrelation(data=y_train, n_lags=24)

# Filter lags exceeding the white-noise significance band
threshold = 1.96 / np.sqrt(len(y_train))

    lag_table
    .loc[lag_table["partial_autocorrelation_abs"] > threshold, "lag"]
    .astype(int)
    .sort_values()
    .tolist()
```
