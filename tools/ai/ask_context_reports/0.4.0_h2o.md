### [grounded] For hourly demand with strong daily and weekly seasonality, when should I prefer a direct forecasting strategy over a recursive one?

- Skills: choosing-a-forecaster; 14.8s

A direct forecasting strategy (using `ForecasterDirect`) is preferable when the predictive relationship between historical observations and the target changes significantly across different lead times in the horizon. It is also favored when error propagation in recursive forecasting degrades performance over long horizons, because direct models do not feed previous predictions back as inputs. However, this comes at the cost of training a separate model for each forecast step.

## When to Prefer Direct Forecasting

* Horizon-dependent patterns: In hourly demand, the dynamics governing a 1-hour-ahead prediction often differ from those for a 24-hour-ahead or 168-hour-ahead prediction. `ForecasterDirect` fits an independent estimator for each specific step, tailoring each model to its exact forecast distance.
* Preventing error propagation: With `ForecasterRecursive`, prediction errors at step 1 feed into step 2, which can compound over multi-step hourly horizons. `ForecasterDirect` uses only observed historical data for all step models, eliminating recursive error accumulation.
* Known, fixed forecast horizon: When operational needs require forecasting a fixed window (such as the next 24 hours or the next 7 days), the requirement to set `steps` at initialization aligns well with the use case.

## Trade-Offs to Consider

* Training time and resource use: `ForecasterDirect` trains N separate models for an N-step horizon, which requires substantially more training time and memory than the single model trained by `ForecasterRecursive`. Training can be accelerated across CPU cores using the `n_jobs` parameter.
* Horizon flexibility: `ForecasterDirect` cannot generate predictions beyond the horizon specified at initialization without re-initializing and retraining the forecaster.
* Starting recommendation: For most time series problems, `ForecasterRecursive` remains the recommended default starting point due to faster training and lower complexity. `ForecasterDirect` is best evaluated when step-specific relationships are suspected or when backtesting indicates that recursive error compounding is harming longer-range steps.

The best lag selection depends directly on the autocorrelation structure, frequency, and seasonality of your specific time series. In skforecast, the recommended approach is to identify candidate lags using partial autocorrelation analysis and confirm the best selection through hyperparameter optimization.

## Methods for Lag Selection

* Partial Autocorrelation Function (PACF): The PACF measures the correlation between the current observation and past observations after removing the linear effects of all intermediate lags. A sharp cutoff after a specific lag typically indicates an autoregressive order, while recurring spikes at fixed intervals indicate seasonal dynamics.
* Lag ranking with `calculate_lag_autocorrelation`: You can rank lags by their predictive potential using `calculate_lag_autocorrelation` from `skforecast.stats`, which orders lags by absolute partial autocorrelation.
* Hyperparameter search: Because the strongest individual lags do not always produce the lowest multi-step forecasting error when combined, candidate sets of lags can be evaluated using `bayesian_search_forecaster`, `grid_search_forecaster`, or `random_search_forecaster`.
