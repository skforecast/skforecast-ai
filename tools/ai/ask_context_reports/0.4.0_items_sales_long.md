### [grounded] For hourly demand with strong daily and weekly seasonality, when should I prefer a direct forecasting strategy over a recursive one?

- Skills: choosing-a-forecaster; 7.9s

Prefer a direct forecasting strategy (`ForecasterDirect`) over a recursive one (`ForecasterRecursive`) when predictive relationships change significantly across the forecast horizon, or when recursive error accumulation degrades accuracy over longer horizons. In hourly demand with daily and weekly patterns, immediate steps often depend heavily on recent momentum, while later steps (such as 24 or 168 hours ahead) rely almost exclusively on seasonal cycles. If your forecast horizon is fixed and you have sufficient computing budget, direct forecasting prevents multi-step error propagation by training an independent model for each step.

## When to Prefer Direct Forecasting

* Horizon-dependent patterns: Use direct forecasting when the relevance of features shifts across the horizon. For example, lag 1 is highly predictive for step 1, but using step 1 predictions recursively to predict step 24 can introduce accumulated noise. Direct models learn step-specific relationships directly from historical data.
* Severe error propagation: In recursive multi-step forecasting, predictions feed back into future lags. If early prediction errors compound and distort daily or weekly peaks and troughs over the horizon, a direct strategy avoids feedback loops entirely.
* Fixed forecast horizons: When operational requirements demand a fixed forecast window (such as a fixed 24-hour day-ahead or 168-hour week-ahead schedule), direct forecasting fits naturally because the number of steps is predetermined.

## Trade-offs to Consider

* Training time and memory: `ForecasterDirect` trains separate estimators for every step in the horizon (for instance, 24 models for a day-ahead horizon or 168 models for a full week). This requires more memory and training time than the single model used by `ForecasterRecursive`, though steps can be trained in parallel using `n_jobs`.
* Horizon flexibility: `ForecasterRecursive` allows predicting any arbitrary number of steps ahead at inference time. `ForecasterDirect` fixes the horizon at initialization, meaning you cannot predict beyond the configured `steps` without retraining.
* Default baseline: `ForecasterRecursive` is the recommended starting point in skforecast. It is best practice to begin with a recursive forecaster and evaluate a direct forecaster via backtesting to confirm whether eliminating error propagation yields an accuracy gain worth the extra training cost.
