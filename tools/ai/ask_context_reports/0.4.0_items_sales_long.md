### [grounded] For hourly demand with strong daily and weekly seasonality, when should I prefer a direct forecasting strategy over a recursive one?

- Skills: choosing-a-forecaster; 12.7s

A direct forecasting strategy (`ForecasterDirect`) should be preferred over a recursive strategy (`ForecasterRecursive`) when the relationship between past observations and future demand changes significantly across the forecast horizon. It is also advantageous when error accumulation across longer horizons degrades recursive performance, provided the forecast horizon is fixed in advance. In hourly demand settings, direct models excel if predicting 1 hour ahead relies on different dynamics than predicting 12 or 24 hours ahead.

## When to Choose Direct Forecasting

* Step-specific relationships: Hourly demand often shows different patterns depending on lead time. For example, immediate persistence may dominate the next 1 to 3 hours, while daily and weekly seasonal cycles dominate hours 12 through 24. `ForecasterDirect` trains an independent model for each step, allowing each estimator to specialize on patterns unique to its specific lead time.
* Preventing error propagation: In a recursive strategy, predictions from earlier steps are fed back as lagged inputs for subsequent steps, which can cause forecast errors to compound over long horizons. A direct strategy trains each step using only observed historical data, completely eliminating recursive error accumulation.
* Fixed forecast horizon: Your operational requirement involves a fixed, predefined forecast horizon (for example, a mandatory 24-hour ahead schedule) that is known when initializing the forecaster.
* Adequate computational budget: You have sufficient compute and memory resources to train and store N separate estimators (one per horizon step), leveraging parallel execution via the `n_jobs` parameter.

## Trade-offs to Consider

* Horizon flexibility: `ForecasterRecursive` trains a single model and can generate predictions for any number of steps at predict time. In contrast, `ForecasterDirect` requires setting the `steps` parameter at initialization and cannot predict beyond that horizon without retraining.
* Training speed and memory: Training N separate models in `ForecasterDirect` requires significantly more compute time and memory than training the single model used by `ForecasterRecursive`.
* Default recommendation: For most standard setups, `ForecasterRecursive` remains the primary starting point. Transitioning to `ForecasterDirect` is recommended when backtesting demonstrates that recursive error propagation degrades accuracy over multi-step horizons, or when lead-time dynamics differ substantially by step.
