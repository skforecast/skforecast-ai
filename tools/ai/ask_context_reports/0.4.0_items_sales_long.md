### [grounded] For hourly demand with strong daily and weekly seasonality, when should I prefer a direct forecasting strategy over a recursive one?

- Skills: choosing-a-forecaster; 11.0s

You should prefer a direct forecasting strategy (`ForecasterDirect`) over a recursive strategy when predictive relationships vary across the forecast horizon or when recursive error propagation degrades multi-step accuracy. In hourly demand settings, predicting 1 hour ahead often relies on immediate momentum, whereas predicting 24 or 168 hours ahead relies primarily on recurring daily and weekly cycles. A direct strategy trains a separate model for each lead time, avoiding error compounding across the horizon.

## When to Prefer Direct Forecasting

* Horizon-dependent patterns: In hourly data, the importance of specific features shifts by lead time. A direct model optimizes an independent estimator for each specific step, tailoring feature weights to that exact horizon.
* Preventing error accumulation: `ForecasterRecursive` feeds earlier predictions back into subsequent steps as lag inputs. Over long horizons (such as a full 24-hour day or 168-hour week), small errors in initial steps compound, whereas `ForecasterDirect` conditions each step solely on observed historical data.
* Fixed operational horizon: When your workflow calls for a fixed planning window (such as a 24-hour day-ahead schedule), the fixed `steps` requirement of `ForecasterDirect` fits the use case without sacrificing flexibility.

## Trade-offs to Consider

* Training compute and memory: `ForecasterDirect` fits N separate estimators (where N is your horizon length), which increases training time and memory usage compared to the single estimator in `ForecasterRecursive`. You can use the `n_jobs` parameter to parallelize training across cores.
* Horizon flexibility: `ForecasterRecursive` can generate forecasts for any horizon length at prediction time, whereas `ForecasterDirect` requires specifying `steps` at initialization and cannot predict beyond that fixed limit.
* Data size: Because direct forecasting fits an independent model for each step, each estimator must have enough training data to learn step-specific dynamics without overfitting.
