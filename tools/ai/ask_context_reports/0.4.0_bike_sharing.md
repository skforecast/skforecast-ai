### [grounded] For hourly demand with strong daily and weekly seasonality, when should I prefer a direct forecasting strategy over a recursive one?

- Skills: choosing-a-forecaster; 17.0s

Direct forecasting with `ForecasterDirect` is preferable when the predictive relationship changes substantially across the forecast horizon, or when recursive error propagation harms accuracy over longer horizons. In hourly demand forecasting, step-specific dynamics (such as immediate autocorrelation dominating early hours versus recurring diurnal cycles dominating later hours) often benefit from training independent models for each step. However, this strategy requires fixing the forecast horizon at initialization and increases computational cost.

## When to Prefer Direct Forecasting

* Horizon-dependent dynamics: Prefer direct forecasting when the effect of features changes by forecast step. For instance, predicting 1 hour ahead relies heavily on immediate recent demand, whereas predicting 24 hours ahead depends more on calendar timing and daily seasonality.
* Mitigating error propagation: In recursive models, predictions feed back into future steps as lagged inputs, which can compound errors across multi-step horizons. Direct models use only observed historical data as inputs for each step-specific model, eliminating error accumulation.
* Extended horizons with distinct patterns: When forecasting across long horizons (such as 24 to 168 hours ahead), direct forecasting ensures that errors in the early steps do not distort forecasts for later hours.

## Trade-offs to Consider

* Fixed horizon requirement: `ForecasterDirect` requires specifying the exact number of `steps` at initialization, whereas `ForecasterRecursive` allows predicting any number of steps at prediction time.
* Training time and memory: Direct forecasting trains one independent estimator per step (such as 24 estimators for a 24-hour horizon), which requires more memory and training time, though training can be parallelized across steps using `n_jobs`.
* Model complexity: Standard practice in skforecast is to start with `ForecasterRecursive` as the default approach, then test `ForecasterDirect` with backtesting to determine whether eliminating error propagation yields better performance for your specific series.
