### [grounded] For hourly demand with strong daily and weekly seasonality, when should I prefer a direct forecasting strategy over a recursive one?

- Skills: choosing-a-forecaster; 15.1s

You should prefer a direct forecasting strategy (`ForecasterDirect`) over a recursive one (`ForecasterRecursive`) when the predictive relationship between past data and the target changes significantly across the forecast horizon, or when recursive error accumulation degrades longer-range forecasts. In hourly demand settings, predicting 1 hour ahead relies heavily on immediate recent lags, whereas predicting 24 or 168 hours ahead depends primarily on seasonal daily and weekly patterns. Direct forecasting avoids error propagation by training an independent model for each step directly from observed data.

## When to Prefer Direct Forecasting

* Horizon-dependent patterns: In hourly demand, the features that matter most change depending on how far ahead you look. Immediate steps (such as step 1 or step 2) rely on recent autoregressive behavior, while later steps (such as step 24 or step 168) rely heavily on daily and weekly seasonality. `ForecasterDirect` trains a dedicated estimator for each horizon step, allowing each step to learn distinct relationships.
* Eliminating error propagation: `ForecasterRecursive` feeds its own predictions back into the model to forecast subsequent steps, which can cause prediction errors to compound over long horizons. `ForecasterDirect` conditions each step strictly on observed history, preventing error accumulation.
* Fixed forecast horizons: If your operations require a fixed horizon (for example, generating a 24-hour day-ahead or 168-hour week-ahead forecast at a scheduled time), the requirement to set `steps` at initialization in `ForecasterDirect` fits naturally into the workflow.

## Trade-offs to Consider

* Training compute and memory: `ForecasterDirect` fits N models (one per step) rather than a single model. For a 24-step or 168-step horizon, training takes longer and consumes more memory, though this can be accelerated using the `n_jobs` parameter.
* Horizon inflexibility: `ForecasterDirect` requires fixing `steps` during forecaster initialization, whereas `ForecasterRecursive` can forecast an arbitrary number of steps at prediction time.

## Identifying Candidate Lags

* Analyze Partial Autocorrelation (PACF): The PACF measures the direct correlation between the series and past time steps after removing the linear effects of intermediate lags. Sharp cutoffs or distinct spikes in the PACF indicate relevant autoregressive terms.
* Rank lags with `calculate_lag_autocorrelation`: You can inspect and rank candidate lags by absolute partial autocorrelation using `skforecast.stats.calculate_lag_autocorrelation`.
* Account for seasonality: Peaks in the autocorrelation function at multiples of a seasonal period indicate that seasonal lags should be tested.
