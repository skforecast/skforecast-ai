### [grounded] For hourly demand with strong daily and weekly seasonality, when should I prefer a direct forecasting strategy over a recursive one?

- Skills: choosing-a-forecaster; 8.3s

You should prefer a direct forecasting strategy (`ForecasterDirect`) over a recursive one when the predictive relationship changes substantially across the forecast horizon or when recursive error accumulation degrades performance. In hourly demand settings, immediate steps often depend heavily on short-term momentum, whereas longer horizons rely primarily on the daily and weekly seasonal cycles. If your operational forecast horizon is fixed in advance, a direct forecaster avoids propagating prediction errors across those steps.

## When to Prefer Direct Forecasting

* Horizon-specific dynamics: In hourly demand, predicting 1 hour ahead relies heavily on the most recent observations, while predicting 24 or 168 hours ahead depends on matching daily and weekly periodicities. `ForecasterDirect` trains an independent model for each step, allowing each estimator to adapt to step-specific relationships.
* Elimination of error propagation: A recursive strategy feeds its own predictions back as lag inputs for subsequent steps, which can compound errors over long horizons. A direct strategy uses only observed historical data as inputs for every step in the horizon.
* Fixed forecasting windows: When business requirements always demand a fixed forecast horizon (such as day-ahead 24-step forecasting), the fixed-horizon requirement of `ForecasterDirect` fits naturally into the operational workflow.

## Trade-offs to Consider

* Training time and memory: Direct forecasting trains separate estimators for each step (for example, 24 estimators for a 24-hour horizon), requiring more memory and compute than the single model trained by `ForecasterRecursive`. You can mitigate training time using the `n_jobs` parameter.
* Horizon flexibility: `ForecasterDirect` requires setting `steps` at initialization and cannot predict beyond that configured horizon, whereas `ForecasterRecursive` can generate arbitrary horizons at prediction time.

## Methods to Identify Optimal Lags

* Partial Autocorrelation Function (PACF): Inspecting the PACF helps identify direct relationships between the target and past values. When the PACF cuts off after lag p, it suggests an autoregressive pattern where lags 1 through p are relevant starting points. You can rank lags by their partial autocorrelation values using `skforecast.stats.calculate_lag_autocorrelation`:

```python
from skforecast.stats import calculate_lag_autocorrelation

lag_table = calculate_lag_autocorrelation(
    data=y,
    n_lags=24,
    sort_by="partial_autocorrelation_abs",
)
```

* Hyperparameter Optimization: Candidate lag sets can be compared directly using backtesting cross-validation. Functions such as `bayesian_search_forecaster`, `grid_search_forecaster`, or `random_search_forecaster` accept candidate lag sets in their parameter grids to determine which combination yields the lowest forecast error.

* Feature Selection: You can define an extended lag window based on domain knowledge or ACF/PACF analysis and then apply `select_features` to prune redundant or uninformative lag features automatically.
