### [grounded] For hourly demand with strong daily and weekly seasonality, when should I prefer a direct forecasting strategy over a recursive one?

- Skills: choosing-a-forecaster; 16.0s

You should prefer a direct forecasting strategy (`ForecasterDirect`) over a recursive strategy (`ForecasterRecursive`) when the relationship between past demand and future values changes significantly across the forecast horizon, such as differing dynamics between 1 hour ahead and 24 hours ahead. A direct strategy is also advantageous when error propagation accumulates substantially over a long horizon, because each step is trained independently using only observed historical data. However, it requires fixing the forecast horizon at initialization and training separate estimators for each step.

## When to Choose Direct Forecasting

* **Step-specific relationships:** In hourly demand forecasting, the features predictive of the immediate next hour often differ from the features predictive of peak demand 12 or 24 hours later. Direct models fit separate estimators for each step, allowing each model to weight inputs specifically for that lead time.
* **Avoiding error propagation:** Recursive forecasting uses its own predictions as lag inputs for subsequent steps, which can compound errors across long horizons. Direct forecasting relies strictly on observed historical inputs for every step model, completely eliminating error feedback loops.
* **Fixed forecast horizons:** If your operational pipeline always forecasts a fixed window (such as a 24-hour day-ahead or 168-hour week-ahead schedule), the fixed-horizon requirement of `ForecasterDirect` fits naturally into the workflow.

## Trade-offs to Consider

* **Training time and resource usage:** `ForecasterDirect` trains N separate estimators for an N-step horizon. For a 24-hour horizon this means 24 models, and for a full weekly horizon (168 hours) it requires 168 models, which increases training runtime and memory footprint compared to the single model used by `ForecasterRecursive`.
* **Parallelization:** Skforecast allows training direct step models in parallel using the `n_jobs` argument, which helps offset the extra compute time if sufficient CPU cores are available.
* **Horizon flexibility:** `ForecasterRecursive` can forecast any arbitrary number of steps at prediction time, whereas `ForecasterDirect` is constrained to the exact `steps` value specified when the forecaster is initialized.
